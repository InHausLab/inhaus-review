Your Home Health Action Guide

Alexis Kaufman  ·  10450 SW 140th Rd, Miami FL 33176  ·  Based on licensed assessment by Jonathan Dickler, MRSA #3550
🚨 URGENT: Black mold (Stachybotrys) confirmed in the nursery. Baby due in approximately 2 months. Start with Step 1 immediately.

What's Going On

A licensed mold assessment of your home found confirmed mold growth and bacteria throughout both of your HVAC systems. Every time your AC runs, it's circulating mold spores into every room in the house.

The most important thing: Stachybotrys — black mold — was detected in your baby's future room. Follow-up testing found the duct insulation feeding that room had an extremely high concentration of mold spores. With your baby arriving in approximately two months, this needs to be your first priority.

The rest of the home showed relatively low airborne mold levels — which is good news. The problem is concentrated in the HVAC system, not hidden throughout your walls. Fix the system, fix the house.

You have time to do this right — but you need to start now.

Your Action Checklist — Work Through In Order
1

Baby's Room — Do This First

👷 Licensed Mold Remediator (MRSR) ⏱ This week — before anything else
  • Remove and dispose of all duct insulation feeding the nursery
  • Clean and disinfect the duct register and surrounding area under proper containment
  • Run a HEPA air purifier in the nursery on HIGH continuously until all work is complete
  • Do not use the room as a nursery until clearance testing confirms it is clean
What to tell the contractor "I need the duct insulation removed from the nursery as a priority. Stachybotrys was confirmed in this room. I need this done under proper containment with negative air pressure."
⚠️ Verify: Florida MRSR License — required by law
2

HVAC Remediation + Full Duct Replacement — Done Together

👷 MRSR Contractor + HVAC Contractor ⏱ Within 2–3 weeks

These two jobs must happen simultaneously under one containment setup. Remediating the HVAC units and replacing the ductwork are a single operation — you don't replace the ductwork first and then blast mold through it while cleaning the units, and you don't clean the units first and then run new ducts while mold is still airborne. One contractor, one containment, done together.

HVAC Unit Remediation:
  • Professional cleaning and disinfection of both evaporator coils
  • Clean and disinfect both blower assemblies
  • Clean and disinfect both drain pans
  • Inspect and treat UV light components on both units
  • Inspect and remove mold-damaged drywall behind HVAC Unit 2
Duct Replacement (same job, same containment):
  • Remove and dispose of ALL existing flexible ductwork on both systems
  • Replace all supply plenums on both units
  • Install R-8 antimicrobial ductwork (ask specifically — carries 10-year mold warranty)
  • Remove and replace all duct insulation in the attic area
  • Restoration-grade cleanup of all work areas before containment is removed
What to tell the contractor "I need HVAC remediation and full duct replacement done at the same time under one containment setup. Both units need blower, coil, and drain pan remediation. I want R-8 antimicrobial ductwork — not R-6. The old ductwork must be bagged and removed. Do not replace the ducts before the units are clean, and do not clean the units before the ducts are ready to go in."
⚠️ Verify: Florida MRSR License for the remediation portion
4

Fix All Bathroom Ventilation

👷 Licensed Electrician or HVAC Contractor ⏱ Before or during duct work

Your exhaust fans are currently venting into the attic — pumping warm humid air directly into the space where your HVAC equipment lives. This is one of the main reasons the attic is a mold problem.

  • Reroute ALL bathroom exhaust fans to vent through the roof to the outside
  • Verify every bathroom has a working exhaust fan — install where missing
  • Install Panasonic-grade quiet exhaust fans
  • Confirm all vent runs terminate outside with a proper exterior vent cap — not into attic
What to tell the contractor "Every exhaust fan in every bathroom needs to vent directly outside through the roof. None should terminate in the attic. Verify all existing runs and re-route any that don't exit the building."
5

Re-Caulk All Bathrooms and Kitchen

👷 Plumber (leak check first) + Handyman ⏱ After plumbing confirmed watertight

Gaps in caulking allow water to seep into walls and floors — creating hidden moisture and future mold. The bathtub access panel showed staining that may indicate a slow leak — check that first.

  • Plumber inspects bathtub access panel for slow leak — fix before caulking
  • Remove all old caulk from every shower (floor edges, wall seams, door frames, fixtures)
  • Remove and replace caulk around all bathtubs
  • Re-caulk all sink countertops, backsplashes, and faucet bases in every bathroom and kitchen
  • Check under all sinks for drips or moisture — repair before caulking
  • Use mold-resistant caulk throughout
What to tell the contractor "I need all bathroom and kitchen caulking replaced with mold-resistant caulk. Before starting, have a plumber check the bathtub access panel — there was staining suggesting a possible slow leak."
6

Install Whole-Home Dehumidification

👷 HVAC Contractor ⏱ As part of HVAC work

In Miami, humidity is your biggest enemy. Mold will return after remediation if humidity isn't actively controlled. Portable units are not enough.

  • Install a whole-home central dehumidifier integrated with your HVAC system
  • Set to run continuously — do NOT use auto mode
  • Install a separate attic dehumidifier (HVAC equipment lives in your attic)
  • Target indoor humidity: 45–50% or below at all times
What to tell the contractor "I need a whole-home dehumidifier integrated with my HVAC, plus a separate attic dehumidifier sized for my attic square footage. Both run continuously."
7

Attic — Roof Repair, Mold Remediation & Insulation Replacement

👷 Roofer + MRSR Contractor + Insulation Contractor ⏱ Must be done in sequence — roof first

Three separate contractors, done in order. Do not skip the sequence — if the roof isn't fixed first, new moisture will undo the remediation work.

Step A — Roofer goes first:
  • Inspect roof at chimney — previous leak confirmed, damaged materials still present
  • Water-test chimney flashing and surrounding area
  • Repair all identified leak points before attic work begins
Step B — Mold remediator second:
  • Remove ALL mold-contaminated insulation (do not clean it — remove it)
  • Treat and clean all affected wood components (rafters, decking, framing)
  • Remove 2 feet past any visible mold or staining
  • Proper containment and negative air pressure throughout
  • HEPA vacuum all surfaces after remediation
Step C — Insulation contractor last (after clearance testing passes):
  • Replace all removed insulation with moisture-resistant Florida-appropriate product
  • Do not install until written clearance from independent MRSA assessor
8

Fix Exterior Drainage

👷 Landscaper or General Contractor ⏱ Lower priority — but don't skip
  • Regrade soil so ground slopes away from foundation (6-inch drop over 10 feet minimum)
  • Repair or replace compromised drainage pans
  • Extend all downspouts at least 6 feet from foundation
  • Clear gutters and confirm water moves away from the home
9

Post-Remediation Clearance Testing — Required Final Step

👷 Independent MRSA Licensed Assessor (NOT the remediation company) ⏱ After all work complete — before baby arrives

Do not accept a contractor's word that work is done. Independent clearance testing is the only way to confirm your home is safe for your baby.

  • Air samples in all rooms including nursery
  • Surface swab samples in all remediated areas
  • Attic clearance testing after insulation replacement
  • Written clearance report from licensed MRSA assessor
  • Indoor levels at or below outdoor levels
What to tell the assessor "I need post-remediation clearance testing following HVAC remediation, duct replacement, and attic mold remediation. I have a baby due in approximately 2 months and need written clearance before the nursery is used."
⚠️ Must be a different company from your remediator

Why All of This Is Connected

Every item on this list addresses one piece of the same problem: moisture getting in and staying in. If any piece is skipped, the conditions for mold growth will return.

Bathroom humidityExhaust fans venting outside

Attic humidityAttic dehumidifier
Indoor humidityWhole-home dehumidifier
Roof leakRoof repair at chimney
Ground waterExterior drainage grading

✅ Verify Before Hiring Any Contractor

  • Florida MRSR license (required for all mold remediation)
  • Florida MRSA license (required for assessment and clearance testing)
  • Proof of liability insurance
  • Written scope of work before work begins
  • Containment plan — negative air pressure required
  • Disposal plan — contaminated materials properly bagged and removed
  • Written completion report after work is done
  • Do NOT use the same company for remediation AND clearance testing
