What the data actually shows

✅ The Good News First

The living-space air quality is mostly fine. The outdoor control measured 270 spores/m³, and most rooms came in below that — some as low as 27/m³. You do not have a whole-house airborne mold crisis. The family is not breathing heavily contaminated air throughout the home.

The problem is concentrated and identifiable: the HVAC systems, the ductwork, and the wall cavities around the baby bath. Fix those, and this house is in good shape.

Your action checklist — work through in order
1

Baby's Room — This Week

👷 Licensed MRSR Mold Remediator  ·  ⏱ Immediately

Active Stachybotrys in the wall cavity and 99,000 spores/cm² on the duct insulation feeding this room. This is the highest-urgency item. Your baby should not sleep in this room until clearance testing confirms it is clean.

  • Remove and dispose of all duct insulation feeding the nursery under proper containment
  • Open and inspect the baby bath wall cavity — remove all mold-affected material
  • Run a HEPA air purifier in the nursery on HIGH continuously until all work is complete
  • Talk to your pediatrician — show them the Stachybotrys finding and lab results
  • Do not use as nursery until written clearance from independent assessor
What to tell the contractor "Stachybotrys was confirmed in the baby bath wall cavity and duct insulation. I need the wall cavity opened and all affected material removed under proper containment with negative air pressure. This is the first priority."
⚠️ Verify: Florida MRSR License — required by law
2

Master Bedroom — Hidden Source Investigation

👷 Licensed MRSR Mold Remediator  ·  ⏱ Same time as Step 1

Pen/Asp at 15x outdoor levels in the master bedroom points to a hidden growth source nearby. This needs to be found and addressed — likely in a wall cavity, under a window, or near a plumbing penetration.

  • Inspect walls around master bedroom — especially below windows and near plumbing
  • Use moisture meter to identify active wet spots
  • Open any identified wet wall cavities and remove mold-affected material
  • Fix the moisture source before closing up walls
3

HVAC Remediation + Full Duct Replacement — Done Together

👷 MRSR Contractor + HVAC Contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 2–3

These two jobs must happen simultaneously under one containment setup. Remediating the units and replacing the ductwork is one operation — you don't replace clean ducts and then blow mold through them cleaning the units, and you don't clean the units with old contaminated ducts still attached.

HVAC Unit Remediation:
  • Professional cleaning and disinfection of both evaporator coils
  • Clean and disinfect both blower assemblies
  • Clean and disinfect both drain pans
  • Inspect and treat UV light components on both units
  • Remove mold-damaged drywall behind HVAC Unit 2
Duct Replacement (same job, same containment):
  • Remove and dispose of ALL existing flexible ductwork on both systems
  • Replace all supply plenums on both units
  • Install new R-8 ductwork (antimicrobial is a reasonable upgrade — see note on upsells below)
  • Remove and replace all duct insulation in attic area
  • Restoration-grade cleanup before containment is removed
What to tell the contractor "I need HVAC remediation and full duct replacement done at the same time under one containment setup. Not cleaning — replacement. Do not replace ducts before the units are remediated, and do not remediate the units with old ductwork still in place."
4

Fix All Bathroom Ventilation

👷 Licensed Electrician or HVAC Contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 2–3

Your bathroom fans currently vent into the attic — pumping warm humid air straight into the space where your HVAC equipment lives. This is a major driver of the attic humidity problem (72% RH confirmed).

  • Reroute ALL bathroom exhaust fans to vent through the roof to the outside
  • Verify every bathroom has a working exhaust fan — install where missing
  • All vent runs must terminate outside with an exterior vent cap — not into the attic
What to tell the contractor "Every bathroom exhaust fan must vent directly outside through the roof. None should terminate in the attic. Check all existing runs and re-route any that don't exit the building."
5

Re-Caulk All Bathrooms and Kitchen

👷 Plumber first, then handyman  ·  ⏱ Weeks 2–3

Moisture readings of 91–100 were recorded near the toilet and bathroom tile — likely an active plumbing leak. Fix the leak first, then seal everything.

  • Plumber inspects bathtub access panel and toilet base — moisture readings of 91–100 indicate active leak
  • Fix any identified leaks before caulking
  • Remove and replace all caulk in every shower, bathtub, sink in every bathroom and kitchen
  • Check under all sinks for slow drips
  • Use mold-resistant caulk throughout
6

Attic — Roof Repair, Mold Remediation & Insulation Replacement

👷 Roofer + MRSR Contractor + Insulation Contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 3–5, in sequence

Three separate contractors, done in strict order. The roof must be fixed before attic remediation begins — otherwise new moisture will undo the work. Insulation goes back in only after clearance testing passes.

Step A — Roofer first:
  • Inspect and water-test chimney flashing and surrounding roof
  • Repair all identified leak points — get written confirmation before attic work begins
Step B — Mold remediator:
  • Remove ALL mold-contaminated insulation — do not clean it, remove it
  • Treat all affected wood components (rafters, decking, framing)
  • Remove 2 feet past any visible mold or staining
  • Proper containment and negative air pressure throughout
  • Improve attic ventilation — address blocked soffits and lack of ridge vents
Step C — Insulation contractor (after clearance testing passes):
  • Replace all removed insulation with moisture-resistant product appropriate for Florida climate
  • Do not install until written clearance from independent MRSA assessor
7

Install Whole-Home Dehumidification

👷 HVAC Contractor  ·  ⏱ As part of HVAC work

In Miami, humidity is permanent and relentless. Without active dehumidification, mold will return. A central whole-home unit integrated with your HVAC is the right long-term solution.

  • Central whole-home dehumidifier integrated with HVAC — run continuously, not on auto
  • Separate attic dehumidifier — HVAC equipment lives up there and needs its own moisture control
  • Target: 45–50% RH or below at all times indoors
💡 Note on upsells A couple of portable HEPA air purifiers and a standalone dehumidifier will cover the interim period cheaply while the remediation work is underway. You don't need to buy the whole-home HEPA system or REME Halo units immediately — those are nice-to-haves, not the fix. See the section below on the remediation proposal.
8

Fix Exterior Drainage

👷 Landscaper or GC  ·  ⏱ Lower priority but don't skip
  • Regrade soil so ground slopes away from foundation (6-inch drop over 10 feet minimum)
  • Repair or replace compromised drainage pans
  • Extend all downspouts at least 6 feet from foundation
  • Clear gutters and confirm water moves away from the home
9

Post-Remediation Clearance Testing — Required

👷 Independent MRSA Assessor — NOT the remediation company  ·  ⏱ After all work is complete

Florida law (Statute 468.8419) requires that assessment and remediation be performed by separate licensed parties. The company that did the work cannot clear their own work. Hire an independent assessor.

  • Air samples in all rooms — including nursery and master bedroom
  • Surface swab samples in all remediated areas
  • Attic clearance after insulation replacement
  • Written clearance report — indoor levels at or below outdoor levels
  • Do not let insulation go back into attic until this passes
What to tell the assessor "I need post-remediation clearance testing. I have a baby due in approximately 2 months and need written clearance before the nursery is occupied. Please include the baby bath wall cavity and master bedroom in the scope."
⚠️ Must be a different company from your remediator — Florida law requires it
A note on the remediation proposal you received

📋 The AQC Proposal — What's Needed vs. What's an Upsell

Air Quality Control Environmental's proposal includes the legitimate work — duct replacement, coil and blower cleaning, removing moldy material under containment. That core scope is real and necessary. But it also bundles in a number of high-margin add-ons that are not part of the remediation and are not what fixes your problem.

✅ What you need from any remediator:

  • Full duct replacement on both systems
  • HVAC unit remediation (coil, blower, drain pan)
  • Baby bath wall cavity — open and remediate
  • Attic mold remediation (after roof is fixed)
  • Mold-damaged drywall removal
  • Proper containment, negative air pressure, documented disposal

⚠️ What to think carefully about before adding on:

🟡
REME Halo Air Purification ($3,000) — An in-duct air purifier. Nice to have, but not remediation. It doesn't fix the contaminated ducts or the moisture sources. Get the core work done first.
🟡
Whole-Home HEPA Filtration ($4,500) — Good for long-term air quality but not urgent. Two portable HEPA purifiers (~$200–300 each) will cover you during and after remediation. Revisit this later.
🔴
Antimicrobial Coating + 10-Year Mold Warranty — Not Supported by Governing Agencies

The EPA, NADCA (National Air Duct Cleaners Association), and the CDC do not recommend or endorse the use of biocide sprays or antimicrobial coatings inside HVAC ductwork as a substitute for proper remediation. The EPA explicitly states: "The use of a biocide... is not routinely recommended."

A "10-year warranty against mold growth" sounds reassuring but is not backed by any regulatory standard. It does not protect you if the roof leaks again, if humidity isn't controlled, or if a new moisture source develops. The warranty is only as good as the conditions it assumes — and mold doesn't read warranties.

What actually prevents mold from returning: fixing the moisture sources. Not a coating.

The R-8 ductwork upgrade itself (better insulation value) is a reasonable choice. The antimicrobial coating sold on top of it is the upsell. Ask your contractor to separate the two in their quote.

🔴
AQC doing their own clearance testing — Florida Statute 468.8419 prohibits the same party from doing both remediation and clearance assessment. AQC should not be clearing their own work. You need an independent licensed MRSA assessor for clearance.
🔴
The proposal doesn't clearly address the roof leak or attic ventilation — these are the actual root causes. Make sure any remediator you hire coordinates with your roofer and addresses attic ventilation as part of the scope.

Get at least one more quote from another licensed MRSR contractor. Present them with this guide and ask them to walk through each item. A good contractor will add their own observations — that's what you want.

🤝 How to Use This Guide With Your Contractors

Share this document with every contractor who walks through your door. Ask them to review each section relevant to their trade and tell you:

  • Do they agree with the scope? Is anything missing?
  • Do they have additional observations based on what they see on-site?
  • What is their specific plan for containment and moisture source remediation?
  • Can they coordinate timing with the other trades involved?
  • Who will handle the post-remediation clearance testing — and confirm it's an independent party?

A good contractor will welcome this conversation. Anyone who dismisses your questions or pressures you to skip steps is a red flag.
