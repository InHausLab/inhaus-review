The open-room air samples do not show a whole-house airborne mold problem at the time of sampling. Most rooms tested at or below outdoor levels — that is reassuring.

However, the HVAC system and duct insulation have confirmed mold growth from lab-tested surface samples. Because the baby is due in approximately two months, the nursery area and HVAC/duct work should begin this week. The nursery should not be occupied until the duct insulation and wall cavity are addressed and independent clearance testing confirms the area is clean.

DO FIRST

Nursery Area — Duct Insulation & Wall Cavity

👷 Licensed MRSR mold remediator  ·  ⏱ This week

The baby bath duct insulation returned 150,000 spores/cm² with confirmed active Cladosporium and bacteria. Trace Stachybotrys was detected in the room air, absent outdoors. The outlet sample at 5,100/m³ is significantly elevated relative to the open room. Given the baby's arrival in approximately two months, this area should be addressed before the room is used as a nursery.

  • Remove and dispose of contaminated duct insulation feeding the nursery under proper containment
  • Have the contractor inspect and assess the baby bath wall cavity — scope of removal to be determined on-site
  • Run a HEPA air purifier in the nursery on HIGH until all work is complete and clearance testing passes
  • Talk to your pediatrician — share the Stachybotrys finding and lab results
  • Do not use the room as a nursery until written clearance from an independent MRSA assessor
What to tell the contractor "The baby bath duct insulation confirmed 150,000 spores/cm² of active mold. Trace Stachybotrys was detected in the room air and was absent outdoors. I need the duct insulation removed and the wall cavity inspected under proper containment. The baby is due in two months — this is the first priority."
⚠️ Verify Florida MRSR license before hiring — required by law
DO FIRST

HVAC System — Stop Normal Operation, Begin Remediation

👷 MRSR contractor + HVAC contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 1–3

All six HVAC surface swabs confirmed active mold growth. Because these materials are in the duct system, operation could distribute spores or fragments into occupied spaces. The EPA states: "Do not run the HVAC system if you know or suspect it is contaminated with mold — it could spread mold throughout the building." The HVAC remediation and duct/insulation work should be done simultaneously under one containment setup.

Contaminated porous duct insulation and flex duct should be removed and replaced. Air handlers, coils, blower assemblies, drain pans, and adjacent affected materials should be professionally evaluated and remediated or replaced as determined by the licensed contractors based on their on-site findings.

  • Do not operate the HVAC system normally until remediation is complete
  • Contractor to assess full extent of contamination — scope of duct replacement based on inspection findings
  • Remove and replace all lab-confirmed and visibly contaminated porous duct insulation/flex duct
  • Remediate both HVAC units: coils, blower assemblies, drain pans, UV light components
  • Assess and address mold-damaged drywall behind HVAC Unit 2
  • All work under proper containment — sealed work area, negative air pressure, HEPA filtration
  • Restore-grade cleanup before containment is removed
What to tell the contractor "All six HVAC surface swabs confirmed active mold. I need the HVAC units remediated and contaminated duct insulation removed and replaced — done simultaneously under one containment setup. Please assess the full extent and advise on scope of duct replacement based on what you find on-site."
DO FIRST

Master Bedroom — Investigate Hidden Source

👷 MRSR contractor  ·  ⏱ Same time as Step 1–2

Master bedroom Pen/Asp measured 200/m³ vs. 13/m³ outdoors — 15x above outdoor levels for that genus. This is a meaningful signal of a localized source nearby, likely a wall cavity, window leak, or plumbing penetration. The contractor should inspect and assess.

  • Inspect walls, window sills, and plumbing areas in master bedroom with moisture meter
  • Identify and address any active wet areas found
  • Scope of remediation to be determined by contractor based on findings
DO NEXT

Fix All Bathroom Ventilation

👷 Licensed electrician or HVAC contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 2–3

Bathroom exhaust fans are currently venting into the attic — pumping humid air directly into the space where HVAC equipment lives. The attic measured 72.7% RH; this is a contributing factor. Fans must vent to the outside.

  • Reroute ALL bathroom exhaust fans to vent through the roof to the outside
  • Verify every bathroom has a working exhaust fan — install where missing
  • All vent runs must terminate outside with an exterior cap — not into the attic
What to tell the contractor "Every bathroom exhaust fan must vent directly outside through the roof. None should terminate in the attic. Check all existing runs and re-route any that don't exit the building."
DO NEXT

Check for Plumbing Leaks & Re-Caulk All Wet Surfaces

👷 Plumber first, then handyman/contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 2–3

Moisture readings of 91–100 were recorded near the toilet and bathroom tile. The bathtub access panel showed staining consistent with a slow leak. Fix any active leaks before caulking — sealing over an active leak traps moisture and accelerates damage.

  • Plumber inspects bathtub access panel, toilet base, and any areas with high moisture readings
  • Fix any identified leaks before caulking
  • Remove and replace all caulk in every shower — floor edges, wall seams, corners, door frames, faucet bases
  • Re-caulk all bathtub perimeters, sink countertops, backsplashes, and faucet bases
  • Re-caulk bathroom window sills where moisture was flagged
  • Check under all sinks for slow drips — repair before caulking
  • Use 100% silicone mold-resistant caulk throughout
Why this matters Gaps in caulking are one of the most common reasons mold returns after remediation. Water enters through a hairline gap, sits behind tile or drywall, and creates a new moisture source. Every wet surface in this home should be confirmed watertight.
DO NEXT

Evaluate Whole-Home Dehumidification

👷 HVAC contractor  ·  ⏱ As part of HVAC work

EPA guidance states indoor RH should be kept below 60%, ideally 30–50%. The attic measured 72.7%. In Miami's climate, active dehumidification is important for long-term mold prevention. Have your HVAC contractor evaluate the right approach for this home's layout.

  • HVAC contractor evaluates whole-home dehumidification options — goal is to maintain indoor RH below 60%, ideally 30–50%
  • Evaluate attic humidity control options — may include dehumidification, ventilation improvements, or both depending on attic type (vented/unvented)
  • If whole-home system is installed, set to run continuously — not on auto mode
On interim measures Portable HEPA air purifiers can be a useful temporary risk-reduction measure while remediation is underway. They are not a substitute for source removal, moisture correction, HVAC remediation, or clearance testing — but running one in the nursery on high until work is complete is a reasonable precaution.
DO NEXT

Attic — Roof Repair, Remediation & Insulation Replacement

👷 Roofer → MRSR contractor → Insulation contractor  ·  ⏱ Weeks 3–5 in sequence

Three contractors, done in strict order. IICRC S520 standard requires moisture sources to be corrected before remediation begins — if the roof isn't fixed first, new moisture will undermine the work. Insulation goes back only after clearance testing passes.

Step A — Roofer first:
  • Inspect and water-test chimney flashing and surrounding roof area
  • Repair all identified leak points — written confirmation before attic remediation begins
Step B — MRSR remediator:
  • Remove all mold-contaminated and moisture-affected insulation
  • Treat affected wood components (rafters, decking, framing) — scope per contractor assessment
  • Proper containment and negative air pressure throughout
  • Evaluate and address attic ventilation (blocked soffits, ridge vents)
Step C — Insulation contractor (after clearance passes):
  • Replace removed insulation — moisture-resistant product appropriate for Florida climate
  • Do not install until written clearance from independent MRSA assessor
DO LATER

📋 The AQC Proposal — Core Work vs. Optional Upgrades

The AQC proposal includes the core remediation scope — duct replacement, coil and blower cleaning, removing contaminated material under containment. That work is real and necessary. The proposal also includes optional indoor air quality upgrades that should be evaluated separately from the remediation.

✅ Core remediation scope (prioritize this):

  • Duct insulation removal and replacement in contaminated areas
  • HVAC unit remediation (coil, blower, drain pan)
  • Baby bath duct insulation and wall cavity assessment
  • Attic mold remediation (after roof is fixed)
  • Proper containment, negative air pressure, documented disposal

⚠️ Optional upgrades — evaluate separately:

🟡
REME Halo Air Purification — An in-duct air purifier. May be a useful long-term addition, but it is not remediation and does not address contaminated duct materials or moisture sources. Not a priority item.
🟡
Whole-Home HEPA Filtration — A good long-term air quality measure, but not urgent remediation. Portable HEPA units can reduce airborne particulates as an interim measure during and after remediation work.
🔴
Antimicrobial Coating + 10-Year Mold Warranty — Not Supported by Governing Standards

The EPA, NADCA (National Air Duct Cleaners Association), and IICRC do not recommend biocide sprays or antimicrobial coatings as a substitute for proper remediation. The NADCA ACR 2021 standard states: "Biocides should not be used for cleaning ductwork. They do not remove allergens and other metabolites from mold, do not kill all mold spores, and may pose health risks to some individuals." The EPA adds that dead mold can still be allergenic — removal, not treatment, is the standard.

A 10-year warranty against mold growth is not backed by any regulatory standard. It does not protect against new moisture sources, roof leaks, or uncontrolled humidity. The R-8 ductwork itself (better insulation value) is a reasonable material choice. Ask the contractor to separate the duct material upgrade from the coating in their quote.

🔴
AQC performing their own clearance testing — Florida law restricts the same party from performing both assessment and remediation on the same structure within the restricted period. Use an independent MRSA-licensed assessor for clearance.

Share this document with every contractor who walks through. Ask them to review each section relevant to their trade and tell you:

  • Do they agree with the scope, or do they see something different on-site?
  • What is their specific containment and moisture correction plan?
  • Can they coordinate timing with the other trades involved?

A good contractor welcomes these questions. Scope may change based on what they find on-site — that is expected and appropriate. This guide sets the direction; licensed contractors determine the final scope.

🚩 Red Flags — If a Contractor Says This, Get a Second Opinion

🔴
"We can do the clearance testing for you too."
Florida law restricts the same party from performing both mold assessment/clearance and mold remediation on the same structure within the restricted period, subject to limited exceptions. Best practice: use an independent licensed MRSA assessor for clearance. If a contractor offers both, ask them to confirm in writing which Florida exception applies.
🔴
"You don't need to fix the roof/ventilation first — our coating handles it."
No coating, spray, or antimicrobial treatment stops water. IICRC S520 standard requires moisture sources to be corrected before remediation begins. A contractor who skips moisture correction is not following the standard of care.
🔴
"We can just clean the ductwork — no need to replace it."
The lab confirmed active growth on porous duct insulation (150,000 spores/cm²). The EPA states contaminated porous materials in HVAC systems should be bagged and removed, not cleaned. Porous insulation that has grown mold cannot be restored to a clean condition by surface treatment alone.
🔴
"This needs to happen today — sign now."
Legitimate remediation requires a written scope, a containment plan, and usually moisture correction first. High-pressure same-day signing is a sales tactic. You have time to get a second quote and review the written scope.
🔴
"Containment isn't necessary for a job this size."
Disturbing mold without containment can spread spores into rooms that currently test clean. Negative air pressure and sealed barriers are standard for mold work per EPA and IICRC guidance. This is not optional.
🔴
They can't show you a Florida MRSR license.
Mold remediation in Florida legally requires an MRSR license. Verify at myfloridalicense.com before work begins. "We've been doing this for years" is not a license.
🔴
Their quote is dramatically cheaper than everyone else's.
A low bid often means skipped containment, no proper disposal documentation, or no moisture remediation. In mold work, the lowest bid is often the one that needs to be redone.
Industry standards & governing guidance

📖 What the Governing Bodies Say

The recommendations in this guide reflect published standards from the agencies that govern indoor air quality and mold remediation in the United States.

🔵
U.S. EPA — Mold Remediation in Schools and Commercial Buildings (EPA 402-K-01-001)
"Do not run the HVAC system if you know or suspect that it is contaminated with mold — it could spread mold throughout the building." The EPA also states: "Contaminated porous materials in the HVAC system should be bagged and removed."
🔵
NADCA ACR 2021 — Assessment, Cleaning and Restoration of HVAC Systems
"Biocides should not be used for cleaning ductwork. Biocides are not recommended for remediation because they do not remove allergens and other metabolites from mold, do not kill all mold spores, and may pose health risks to some individuals."
🔵
IICRC S520 — Standard for Professional Mold Remediation
"The cause(s) of mold growth must be identified and corrective action taken before remediation of either the building or the HVAC system is undertaken." Fixing the roof and moisture sources before remediating is the published standard of care, not a preference.
🔵
EPA — Indoor Humidity Guidance
"Keep indoor relative humidity below 60%; ideally between 30% and 50%." The attic in this home measured 72.7% RH — well above the threshold at which mold growth is actively supported.
🔵
Florida Statute 468.8419 — Mold-Related Services
Florida law restricts the same licensed party from performing both mold assessment and mold remediation on the same structure within the restricted period. For this project, use an independent licensed MRSA assessor for post-remediation clearance.
