We've built a system that automatically scores every inspection 0–100, tracks performance per inspector over time, and ties compensation to outcomes — not just hours worked. Inspectors see their score before they submit. They see a clock. They see a dollar amount. It changes behavior.
How the Score Works
Calculated automatically the moment an inspector submits. No manual review required.
📸
Photo Accountability
30 points
Every photo must have a decision: assign it to a section, mark it as not needed, or save it for later. Inspectors don't have to use every photo — but they can't leave photos sitting there with no decision made.
🔍
Observations
25 points
Each observation needs a room location, a written note, and at least one photo. Partial credit for text without a photo reference.
🔧
Actions Taken
25 points
Every action taken on-site (filter replaced, equipment installed) needs a description and supporting photo. Full credit for both, partial for description only.
✅
Checklist Gates
20 points
Room notes reviewed, test locations recorded, sample IDs captured, all photos marked include/exclude, report notes filled in.

How the Bonus Diminishes
Rewards speed AND quality. Can't game one without the other.
1
First 4 hours — Full bonus ($50)
Inspector wraps up and submits within 4 hours of starting. Score must be 85+. They get the full same-day bonus. This is the target behavior — efficient, thorough, done.
2
4–6 hours — Reduced bonus ($50)
Still same day, still A grade, but took a bit longer. Bonus is there — just smaller. Creates a real incentive to not drag it out.
3
6–8 hours — Minimum bonus ($20)
Getting to the end of the day. Still worth finishing strong. $20 is still real money — it's worth it to place those last photos and get the score above 85.
4
After 8 hours or next day — No bonus
Window is closed. Base comp still applies. Monthly average still counts. But the same-day urgency is gone. Inspector learns: do it right, do it fast, or leave money on the table.
Why This Changes Behavior
It's not just a scorecard — it's a feedback loop that runs during every inspection.
💡
Inspectors self-correct in real time
They see "4 photos need a decision" and "score is 71 — need 85 to qualify." They go back and resolve each one — assign it, mark it not needed, or save it for the report. Without this, photos with no decision end up dumped on Tanner.
📈
Performance is objective, not subjective
No more "I think Dave did a good job this month." The score is the score. 91 is 91. Every inspector knows exactly what earns more money and what triggers a PIP. No surprises.
🏆
Good inspectors get rewarded, bad ones self-select out
An inspector who consistently scores 90+ earns meaningfully more than one who scores 60. Over time, you attract quality people and the low performers either improve or leave. The system does the work.
